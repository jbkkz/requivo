# Decision Brief

> What to review before committing to the scope — generated by Requivo

## Request and objective

**Objective:** Replace the informal email-based leave approval process — where requests get lost or stall for weeks and HR only discovers unapproved leave at payroll — with a workflow where every leave request reaches an explicit, recorded, traceable decision, enforcing HR sign-off for long or over-balance requests and producing a full audit trail for works-council compliance.
**Problem:** Leave decisions get lost in email and are never reliably recorded, so HR only discovers unapproved absence at payroll.
**Solution:** A traceable leave-approval workflow with conditional HR sign-off, nightly balance sync with the legacy HR tool, and a compliance-grade audit trail.
**Complexity:** medium — conditional two-stage approval with an escalation timer; bidirectional nightly sync and conflict/mismatch handling with an external system; delegation-by-date-range on top of the reporting-line permission model; 5-year audit trail with works-council-grade completeness
**Main cost driver:** the nightly sync and balance-mismatch reconciliation logic with the legacy HR tool

## Current understanding

A Leave/Absence request lifecycle: submitted → manager review → HR review (mandatory above 10 working days or over-balance) → approved/rejected/cancelled. Named-substitute delegation and 3-day auto-escalation to HR ensure no request stalls silently. Nightly bidirectional sync with the legacy HR tool (source of truth for balances during the pilot): approvals and cancellations write back same-night; cancellations auto-restore balance without HR confirmation; over-balance approvals do require HR confirmation. Requests submitted during an unresolved balance mismatch proceed against the last-known balance, flagged at-risk, never blocked — HR resolves the mismatch the next morning. Full audit trail (who approved/overrode/escalated/cancelled, when) retained 5 years. Single shared rule set for the pilot, no per-entity configuration yet.

## Decisions made

- **Manager approves by default; HR sign-off is mandatory above 10 working days or for over-balance requests** — Keeps routine approvals fast while routing higher-risk or exception cases to HR.
  - _Alternative weighed:_ All requests could route through HR, or all could be manager-only regardless of duration/balance.
  - _Trade-off accepted:_ Faster turnaround for the common case, at the cost of a conditional branch in the workflow that must stay correctly enforced.
- **Cancellations auto-restore balance same night without HR confirmation** — Cancellations reduce risk (give balance back), unlike over-balance approvals which increase it.
  - _Alternative weighed:_ Require HR confirmation symmetrically with over-balance approvals.
  - _Trade-off accepted:_ Faster, lower-friction cancellations, accepting that balance restoration happens without a human check.
- **Requests during an unresolved mismatch proceed against last-known balance, flagged at-risk, never blocked** — Prioritizes the 'a decision always happens' objective over strict balance accuracy at submission time.
  - _Alternative weighed:_ Block new requests until HR resolves the mismatch.
  - _Trade-off accepted:_ Keeps the process moving and avoids employee-facing stalls, at the cost of approving some requests against potentially stale data.
- **Single shared workflow and rule set for the pilot, no per-department or per-country configuration** — Reduces pilot scope and lets the core loop be validated before investing in configurability.
  - _Alternative weighed:_ Build per-department/country rule variation from the start.
  - _Trade-off accepted:_ Faster pilot delivery, deferring rework if different departments or countries need distinct rules later.
- **Stalled requests with no named backup approver auto-escalate to HR after 3 working days** — Guarantees no request silently stalls indefinitely, matching the core traceability objective.
- **Full audit trail retained 5 years for works-council compliance**

## Scope implications

- a multi-step conditional approval engine (manager, then HR above a threshold or over-balance)
- a bidirectional nightly sync and conflict/mismatch-detection layer against the legacy HR tool
- a date-ranged delegation mechanism layered on top of the reporting-line permission model
- a scheduled escalation/stall-detection job running independently of user action
- a compliance-grade, 5-year audit trail surface for works-council reporting
- a daily HR operational queue for resolving overnight balance mismatches

## Assumptions worth contesting

### Zero-mismatch goal vs nightly sync
- **Premise:** The success metric demands zero unresolved balance mismatches at month end, while the design explicitly allows mismatches to occur every night and lets new requests proceed against stale balances rather than blocking.
- **Alternative:** Reasoning from the design itself: since mismatches are an accepted, recurring by-product of nightly (not real-time) sync, the metric should be 'resolved within 24h', or the over-balance check specifically should call the legacy tool live instead of relying on the nightly batch.
- **Consequence:** As stated, hitting 'zero mismatches at month end' depends entirely on HR never missing a morning reconciliation — one lapse fails the pilot's own bar even though the system behaved exactly as designed.
- **Recommendation:** Reconcile the success metric's wording with the async sync design before acceptance testing begins.

### No enforcement against email approvals
- **Premise:** The request treats 'a decision that always happens' as solved once the workflow exists, but nothing in scope stops a manager from simply replying to an email as before.
- **Alternative:** Pair the launch with an explicit organizational rule — e.g. payroll only accepts system-recorded decisions, and HR flags/reports any email-based approval it becomes aware of — since software alone cannot force the behavior change the client actually wants.
- **Consequence:** A functionally correct system that managers route around by email reproduces the exact problem being solved, while still passing every test written against the workflow logic.
- **Recommendation:** Define the policy/communication lever that makes the platform the only valid approval channel, and add it to acceptance criteria alongside the functional tests.

### Legacy tool as permanent truth source
- **Premise:** The legacy HR tool is named as authoritative 'during the pilot,' implying a temporary state, but no decision exists on what happens to balance ownership after the pilot.
- **Alternative:** Decide now whether pilot success triggers migrating balance ownership into the new platform, or whether the nightly-sync dual-system dependency is meant to persist — because an undecided 'temporary' architecture tends to become the permanent one by default.
- **Consequence:** Left open, the mismatch-handling and nightly-sync logic being built for a pilot becomes the long-term architecture, baking in a recurring reconciliation cost indefinitely.
- **Recommendation:** Get an explicit post-pilot integration target from the client before finalizing the sync design.


## Main risks

- The 'zero mismatch' success metric silently depends on HR resolving every mismatch every single morning without exception.
- The platform's shared approval-circuit/permission framework (used across modules) likely needs extending for delegation-by-date-range and HR override — a change here can ripple into other workflows.
- A bug in nightly conflict resolution corrupts balance data that payroll ultimately trusts, with no real-time cross-check as a safety net.
- 5-year audit retention for works-council compliance may collide with GDPR erasure requests on the same HR data, and no resolution is defined.
- Adoption risk: the tool only solves the stated problem if managers genuinely stop approving by email, which nothing in the build enforces.

## Unresolved questions

- Whether the 'zero mismatch' success metric should be redefined against the nightly-sync design
- How adoption is enforced so managers stop approving by email outside the system
- Post-pilot ownership of the balance source of truth (stay on legacy tool vs. migrate)
- Full field-level definition of the Leave/Absence business object
- Least explored: The named problem is not 'requests are annoying to submit' but 'decisions silently fail to happen' — meaning adoption enforcement (managers genuinely stop using email) matters as much as the workflow logic itself; a technically correct system that managers route around by email again would fail the actual objective even while passing every functional test.
