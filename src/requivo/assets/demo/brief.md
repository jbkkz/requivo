## Request and objective

**Objective:** Give door staff live, reliable visibility into guest approval status during check-in, and give finance a clean, auditable link between freelancer hours worked, signed contracts, and invoiced amounts — eliminating both wrongful admissions/rejections at the door and weeks of post-event invoice reconciliation.
**Problem:** Nobody can see live guest-approval status at the door, and freelancer hours, contracts and invoices don't reconcile after the event.
**Solution:** An offline-capable door check-in app showing live approval status, paired with a freelancer hours/contract/invoice reconciliation layer that flags mismatches for finance.
**Complexity:** high — offline snapshot + sync with conflict resolution across multiple devices; two structurally different rule sets bundled under one 'mismatch' concept; unclear integration with an existing contract/invoicing system; six-week fixed deadline with no room for schedule slip
**Main cost driver:** the offline sync/conflict-resolution layer plus reconciling two disparate rule sets within a fixed six-week window

## Current understanding

Likely two connected sub-systems: (1) an offline-capable check-in app for door staff showing live attendee approval status, with local snapshot + sync given the venue's unreliable wifi; (2) a freelancer time/contract/invoice reconciliation layer that flags mismatches (hours vs. invoice vs. signed contract) and alerts a responsible person, feeding a clean reconciliation report to finance at close.

## Assumptions worth contesting

### One word, two different problems
- **Premise:** The request treats 'mismatch' as a single alert covering both guest approval and freelancer hours-vs-invoice discrepancies.
- **Alternative:** Event-ops and back-office reconciliation are structurally different builds — different data models, different urgency (real-time at the door vs. post-event for finance), different owners (door staff vs. finance).
- **Consequence:** Designing one undifferentiated 'mismatch' concept risks a shallow build that satisfies neither: a door alert that's too noisy to act on, and a finance report that still doesn't tie out.
- **Recommendation:** Split into two explicit rule sets and two acceptance criteria before writing any workflow — attendee approval mismatch, and hours/contract/invoice mismatch — each with its own owner and channel.

### Clocking freelancer hours is a legal tripwire
- **Premise:** The build assumes freelancer hours can simply be tracked and reconciled against invoices like a timesheet.
- **Alternative:** Under French labor law, freelancers who are clocked in/out and monitored like employees are a classic fact pattern for requalification as disguised employment (salariat déguisé) — freelance invoicing is normally tied to a deliverable or mission, not hours worked.
- **Consequence:** If the app effectively timestamps freelancer arrival/departure and drives invoice amounts from those hours, the client may be building evidence against their own contractor classification.
- **Recommendation:** Check with legal/HR before building an hours-based reconciliation — confirm whether the 'mismatch' should be against mission/deliverable terms in the contract, not clocked hours.

### Six weeks, two builds, no rehearsal slack
- **Premise:** The plan treats offline check-in and freelancer/invoice reconciliation as one project deliverable on the same six-week clock.
- **Alternative:** The event date can't move, so the check-in path — the one thing that fails visibly in front of the client — should get the depth and rehearsal time, while reconciliation could ship as a clean post-event report rather than a live alert.
- **Consequence:** Splitting effort evenly across both risks an under-rehearsed door experience, which is the one failure mode with no second chance.
- **Recommendation:** Sequence and scope explicitly: harden and rehearse check-in first; de-scope freelancer reconciliation to a post-event reconciliation report unless real-time alerting is proven necessary.
